Bri's Booking Hacks

Italy sounds expensive, but it can be done on a budget. Plus, Southern Italy is cheaper than anything north of Rome. It doesn't have to break the bank, and we want you there, so here are some tips.

Ballpark budget: flight + hotel bundled together can run around $2,700 on a budget or $3,500+ mid-tier per couple. That's before food and activities for 7–8 days. Luxury goes up from there. For a deeper breakdown of costs, read this article.

Where to Stay

We have a room block at the Grand Hotel President, and we'd love to have you there with us. We also know a lot of you may want to stay with your family in a VRBO or find a more affordable hotel, and that is fantastic. Come however works for you! Block details are on the Where to Stay page.

If you're booking on your own, here's what I'd do:

  • Book a free-cancellation rate, then keep watching. Lock in a room early, check the price every few weeks, and rebook if it drops. This is my favorite hotel hack.
  • Track hotel prices like you track flights. Google Hotels lets you turn on price tracking for your dates, so the drops come to you.
  • Look one town over. Sant'Agnello and Piano di Sorrento are right next door, often cheaper, and on the local train line.
  • Split a villa with family or friends. For a group, a VRBO can cost less per person than hotel rooms, and you get a kitchen and a terrace for your morning espresso.
  • Count the cost of getting around. A cheaper room farther out can end up costing more once you add taxis to and from the wedding events.
  • Look for "Breakfast Included." Italian breakfasts are lovely, and having one meal covered every day helps your money go farther.

Flights

When to book: I start looking in January or February out of personal habit. Lots of people stop shopping for travel after the holidays, and I've found great deals that way. Traditionally, though, fares to Europe are lowest when you book at least 72 days out and tend to climb after that. Our RSVP deadline is June 2027, so our advice: RSVP by June, and have your flights booked by the end of July.

  • Set a price alert. Search your dates on Google Flights and turn on price tracking for the flight you want. Prices shift, so don't assume the first price you see is the final one.
  • Use your miles and points. If you've built up airline miles, this is the trip to put them toward. Also check your credit card to see if you've accumulated points you can put toward your flight or hotel.
  • Fly into Rome. It's generally a more affordable entry point than flying directly into Naples. Details on getting from Rome to Sorrento are on the Getting There & Around page.
  • Play with your dates. Shifting your departure or return by a day or two can change the price a lot. Round trip is usually cheaper than two one-ways.
  • Don't fear the layover. A stop usually lowers the price, and sometimes you can grab a long layover in Amsterdam or Dublin (if you fly Aer Lingus!) and explore a new city on the way.
  • Use the 24-hour rule. US rules let you cancel most tickets for free within 24 hours of booking, as long as you book at least a week before the flight. If the price drops the next morning, cancel and rebook.
  • Skip Basic Economy for this one. It's cheaper, but usually can't be changed. If plans shift, that money is gone.
  • Check out vacation packages. Some airlines bundle your flight and hotel into one package. It can be a decent deal, but it isn't always, so compare it with booking each piece separately. Here's how we do it:

How We Book It: Delta Vacations

We've used Delta Vacations for our own trips and keep coming back to it. For our MSP folks, it's a great option. For our Buffalo folks, United has a similar program, but I'm not as familiar with it. You bundle your flight and hotel into a single package and a single payment, instead of juggling two separate bookings and two separate bills.

The part we really like: you put down a deposit to lock in the package, then chip away at the rest on your terms, say $300 or $400 a month, however often works for you. By the time you actually travel, most or all of the trip is already paid off. All that's left to spend once you land is food and whatever you want to buy while you're there.

Pay it off a couple of months early and you can start setting aside a separate spending budget instead, so you land with the trip covered and cash ready to go, not scrambling to catch up when you get home.

When booking, look for an airport transfer (not always possible) and "Breakfast Included."

One more tip: take note of how much your flight costs the day you book. If you watch it go down a month later, you can call them and they'll give you e-credits for the future. They require you to book at least a 3-night stay through them, so you can book a different hotel on your own for part of the trip (like when you're switching cities), or switch hotels within the same trip through them too. I've had three hotels on my itinerary with them before and it works out great!

Travel insurance: you can add it right through Delta Vacations when you book. One nice perk: it lets you cancel and get a refund up to 30 days before takeoff. Since you're booking this far in advance, that's a lot of runway for peace of mind if plans change. United Vacations packages offer this as well.

Before You Go

  • Call your cell phone carrier. Ask about international plans, or make sure your phone is unlocked if you want to use an eSIM instead. It's usually cheaper and easier than paying for a full international plan.
  • Check your passport now. Make sure it's current and won't expire within 6 months of your travel dates.
  • Get a cross-body or anti-theft bag. Something that keeps your money, cards, and passport close to your body. Pickpocketing happens in busy tourist areas, and a secure bag is a small thing that saves a lot of stress.
  • Back up your passport and credit cards. Take a photo of both sides of each and email it to yourself, plus keep a physical photocopy in a separate bag from the originals. If anything gets lost or stolen, you'll still have what you need to get money and get home.
