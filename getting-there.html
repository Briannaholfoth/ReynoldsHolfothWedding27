Getting There & Around

First things first: however you get here, you'll get here. Below is everything we know, organized so you can read as much or as little as you need.

The short version: Fly into Naples, book a private transfer, done. That's the easy path. Keep reading for the slightly more adventurous version.

Which Airport?

If this is your first time in Italy, fly into Rome. There's a reason everyone says you have to see it, and it would be a shame to skip it on your one trip. If you can't swing the extra time, Naples is closer to us and gets you here faster.

If You Fly Into Rome

Do not rent a car. Rome is walkable (very walkable, it's the NYC of Italy) and driving in the city is genuinely stressful. In Brandon's words, "it's the only city I've ever only walked uphill the entire time" (it's the city of seven hills, so he's not wrong). If you decide you want a car for the rest of your trip, rent it on your way out of Rome, not while you're in it.

Heading to Rome? Check out the Extend Your Trip page for our recommendations.

Rome to Naples

Take the train. It's 1 hour 13 minutes versus about 2.5 hours by car, and we don't recommend driving in Rome anyway. Trenitalia and Italo both run the high-speed line between Roma Termini and Napoli Centrale, both have apps so your ticket lives on your phone, and Italo tends to run a little cheaper.

How Italian train stations actually work (this trips up almost every first-timer): you don't need to show up two hours early like you would for a flight. Thirty to 45 minutes is plenty. Your specific train number won't appear on the departure board until about 15 to 20 minutes before it leaves. The board only shows the next handful of trains, so if you're standing there an hour early, you might see a train to your destination on the board with a completely different number than the one on your ticket. That's normal, it just means yours hasn't posted yet. Relax, grab a coffee, and check back closer to your departure time.

Arriving in Naples: The One Confusing Part

Napoli Centrale (the big station where your high-speed train lands) and Naples Airport are two completely different places, don't mix them up. If you're coming from the airport, the Alibus shuttle runs to Napoli Centrale for about €5 and takes 15 to 30 minutes, or you can grab a taxi.

Here's the genuinely tricky part: to continue on to Sorrento, you have to go underground within Napoli Centrale to a smaller station called Piazza Garibaldi. It's technically part of the same complex, just one level down, but it switches names on you, which catches everyone off guard the first time. From there you'll take the Circumvesuviana line to Sorrento (or hop off at Pompeii first if you want to explore, there are luggage lockers at that stop).

  • Circumvesuviana: about €4.40–5, roughly 70–80 minutes, runs every 30 minutes. Regular commuter train, no AC, gets crowded. A little more "local," passing through some rougher-looking neighborhoods, so if you want a slice of real, unpolished Naples, this is it.
  • Campania Express: about €15, roughly 60–70 minutes, air conditioned, dedicated luggage space, guaranteed seating. Runs seasonally (roughly March through October). Bookable online in advance.
A safety note, because we'd rather you hear it from us: the underground station area and the regular Circumvesuviana trains are known hotspots for pickpockets. Keep your bag zipped and in front of you, especially with luggage. It's a totally normal part of traveling in a busy city, not a reason to worry, just a reason to stay a little sharper than usual. Every slightly confusing or frustrating moment on this trip becomes a great story later, promise.

If any of this sounds like more than you want to deal with, this is exactly when to book a private transfer instead. No shame in the easy way.

There are plenty of other private drivers too. If you have credit card travel points, check your card's travel portal first, you can often book transfers or hotels through there for free or close to it. And if you don't have points yet, booking travel on a card that earns them is a nice way to start (pay it forward to future-you).

Renting a Car or a Vespa

We personally won't rent a car (walking and trains cover almost everything), but it's a completely fine choice if you'd rather have one, we'd just recommend picking it up in Naples rather than Rome. A Vespa is also a fun option and you can rent one right in Sorrento for a day or two, it's one of those iconic Italy things.

We'll be taking the train and a private transfer ourselves, and renting a Vespa for a couple of days to explore Amalfi and Sorrento just for fun.
