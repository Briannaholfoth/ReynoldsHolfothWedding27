A Little Bit of Us

Why We Travel

If you know us, you know we love to travel, and we're not shy about finding a good deal to do it. There are so many ways to see the world without draining your savings account, and honestly, we think that's part of the fun.

But travel isn't really about the deal-hunting for us. It's about what it does to your head. Every trip hands you a slightly different version of what life could look like, different creativity, different pace, different people who see the world in a way you never considered. It's the fastest perspective shift we know.

"Travel is about the gorgeous feeling of teetering in the unknown." — Anthony Bourdain

That's the whole thing, really. You don't know the language. You don't know the currency. You don't fully know the customs, no matter how much you prepped. Every trip abroad is a small risk, and we've come to believe no risk means no reward.

The best parts haven't come from the trips that went perfectly either. They've come from the missed museum because a storm rolled in, the flight that got delayed, the plan that fell apart and turned into something better. That's where the actual growth happens, not the five-star stuff (though we don't mind that either).

And then there's the food. We're foodies, unapologetically. Bourdain believed food was never just food, it carried a place's whole history, its people, its grandmothers. We believe that too, which is a big part of why a trip built around a big Italian meal with the people we love felt like the only way to do this.

His advice, more or less, was to move: as far as you can, as much as you can, across an ocean or just across a river, eating what the locals eat and walking around in their shoes for a while.

"Drink heavily with locals." — Anthony Bourdain

Honestly, can't argue with that one.

We hope this trip gives you a little bit of that same teetering, gorgeous unknown. Prego.
