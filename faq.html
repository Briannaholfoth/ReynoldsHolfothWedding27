Good to Know

FAQ

What should I expect from this trip?

Let's be real: this isn't a resort-and-done kind of trip. There's a long flight, train travel, a currency and language that aren't your own (though most Italians speak English), and a few logistics to sort out. It takes a little effort. But that effort makes the trip all the more worthwhile. Come ready for a picturesque adventure.

How should I prepare?

Get your body ready as much as your suitcase, there's a lot of walking, hills, and stairs. Hiking and general mobility work in the months before helps more than you'd think. Brandon's advice: "Put some bowling balls in your suitcase and practice carting it up and down stairs." The hills there aren't a joke.

What apps should I download before the trip?

A handful of these will make the whole trip smoother:

  • Google Translate — download this first. Reads menus, street signs, and helps you communicate even when the phrasebook fails you.
  • Trenitalia or Italo — whichever train line you end up booking, get the app so your tickets live on your phone. See Getting There & Around for details on which to use.
  • Offline maps — download the map areas for Rome, Naples, and Sorrento in your maps app before you go, so you're never stuck without directions if you lose signal.
  • WhatsApp — free international calls, and how most of Italy (and Europe generally) communicates. Restaurants often want reservations made through it, and it's how you'll reach Anna Angela too.

Will I need a visa?

No visa, but you will need an ETIAS travel authorization, a quick, inexpensive online screening the EU is rolling out for visa-exempt countries. It's expected to be required by the time of the wedding. We'll update this page with the application link once it's live.

What's the weather like?

Highs around 74°F, lows around 63°F. Layers recommended. Earlier in October tends to be nicer than later in the month.

Are kids invited?

We recommend leaving little ones at home if it means you can still make it, but if that's the deciding factor, talk to us, we want you there above all else.

Can I bring a plus one?

Ask Bri or Brandon directly, it'll most likely be a yes.

Do I really need to pay to use the bathroom? And pay for water?

YES! That's why you'll always want some Cash/coins on you. Train stations require you to pay. Restrooms are free on the trains. Pro tip: Look for the golden arches! You can always go for free in a McDonalds. Regarding water, you will get charged at every resturant for it. Rome has fountains randomly throughout the city that are safe to drink and are free, but they are hard to find.

Any tips on language, money, and culture?

Number 1: take some time to research the culture and where you want to go, it's fun! There are so many ways to learn about Italian culture, it's wise to do some investigating before departure. Main takeaways: They tend to eat later for many meals. They take their time at meals and the waitstaff won't automatically bring you the check, you must ask for it. Modest dress code in churches. No beachware in restuarants. Riposino: Mid-day break where some places shut down. Don't touch market product. Saying "Good morning" or "hello" go a long way!

Number 2:Hit your bank 3 weeks before you leave and convert some cash to euros, most of Europe runs on card, but you'll want cash on hand for tips (drivers, tour guides, the occasional Uber). For a week, €200 is plenty. For 8–10 days, bring €300–400. Europe isn't a tipping culture, but America is. We stick out like sore thumbs, so sometimes they do expect it. Also, if you receive excellent service and you feel it's appropriate, then tip.

Number 3: Learn some basic Italian phrases. Plenty of free language apps, or just keep a cheat sheet in your phone notes of the basic ones to review before you go out. It's respectful to try and they appreciate it.

Number 4: Get comfortable walking shoes! Being stylish is sooo Italian, extra points for that!

Number 5: Speaking of fashion, shopping there is pretty great! Currently, the Euro is strong, so prices will be about the same. Italians are fashionable (Milan is a fashion Mecca afterall!) so you may find some high quality garments. Pro Tip: Bring an extra compressable bag with you so you can fill it with Souvenirs. Saves you from buying one there (you think you won't, you will) and you can easily check it on the way home!

There is so much to see, how do I decide? Planning is overhwelming!

Bri is here to help! Late winter is Bri's favorite time to plan for trips, feel free to ask her questions. Otherwise, list 3 things you MUST see/do while you're there. Plan around that (and the wedding obviously). European vacations tend to be busier because there is so much to see in such little time. Just know you WON'T see it all, so pick what's most important and leave plenty of time to wander and roam aimlessly to stumble upon something magical.
